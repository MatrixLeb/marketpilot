Safety & compliance

MarketPilot is built to make the cautious path the default path. This chapter is the exact model: what is on at first launch, what the scheduler refuses to do, and which legal responsibilities remain yours.

The defaults are deliberately boring. You can loosen them, but you have to mean it: no hidden “skip for this one” path, no silent retry that outlives a broken token, and no way to reach a suppressed contact through the normal send flow.

This is product documentation, not legal advice. Cold email, WhatsApp marketing and newsletters are regulated differently in different countries. The app enforces mechanics; your business remains responsible for the lawful basis, the sender identity and the content.

Dry run, precisely

What it stops:

  • Social API publish calls. The post becomes SIMULATED with “Dry run — nothing was sent.”
  • SMTP sends and WhatsApp template or text messages. Outreach messages become SIMULATED.
  • Manual outbox bundles. No folder is written for a simulated Manual post.

What it does not stop:

  • AI generation. Your configured provider still receives the brief and brand context.
  • Test connection calls. Those are read-only checks and do not publish.
  • Drafting, scheduling, CSV import and local artwork rendering.

Simulated posts still count towards the daily cap and minimum gap. That is intentional: a dry-run test should behave like the real queue, including its limits.

--dry-run on the CLI is persistent. It writes the same saved switch as the top-bar chip, so future scheduled ticks stay simulated until you turn dry run off in the app. See CLI flags.

Guard rails on every publish

Before an approved post leaves the queue, the scheduler checks:

CheckFailure behaviour
Inside the brand's sending windowHeld with “outside 08:00–20:00” (or your window). The post stays approved for a later tick.
Daily cap for this channelHeld. Simulated posts from earlier today count.
Minimum gap since the last postHeld until enough minutes have passed.
Status still approvedClaimed atomically as PUBLISHING before the API call.

Failures are classified. Rate limits and server errors retry after 15 minutes, then 30, with a maximum of three attempts. Authentication, permission and validation errors stop immediately and leave the post FAILED with the API message.

Outreach gates

A campaign can prepare or send a message only when all of the following are true:

  • The campaign is ACTIVE.
  • The lawful-basis confirmation is ticked.
  • The current time is inside the brand's sending window.
  • The campaign's daily cap has room.
  • The lead is not do-not-contact, not unsubscribed and not on the suppression list.
  • The lead has a valid email, or a phone number with at least eight digits for WhatsApp.
  • Newsletters additionally require Subscribed.

One contact per campaign

MarketPilot deliberately does not do drip sequences or automatic follow-ups. Once a lead has a message record in a campaign — drafted, sent, skipped or failed — that lead is not queued again for the same campaign. To follow up, create a new campaign on purpose.

The suppression list

Pressing Unsubscribe on a lead sets do-not-contact, clears subscribed, and adds the normalised email and phone to a permanent suppression list. Every prepare and send path checks that list. Deleting the lead does not remove the suppression entry.

A suppression entry can only be removed by editing the workspace file directly. Do that only when the person has explicitly asked to be contacted again and you have a lawful basis.

Email mechanics

Outreach and newsletter email is plain text. Each message includes:

  • The sender name, postal address and a visible unsubscribe line.
  • A List-Unsubscribe mailto header and a one-click List-Unsubscribe-Post header.
  • Recipients in BCC, with the From address in the To field.

Replies to the unsubscribe mailbox are not processed automatically. Read them and press Unsubscribe on the lead. WhatsApp messages append “Reply STOP to opt out.”; honour those replies the same way.

Your compliance checklist

Before the first outreach campaign:

  • You know why you may contact each person: consent, a legitimate-interest assessment, an existing customer relationship, or another basis your jurisdiction recognises.
  • The sender identity is real and the From address belongs to your business.
  • The postal address and unsubscribe path are accurate and monitored.
  • The list contains only contacts you obtained lawfully — not a purchased or scraped list.
  • You have checked whether B2B rules, opt-in rules or a prior-consent requirement apply in the recipient's country.

Before every send:

  • Read a sample of the messages. The writer can be wrong about a price, service or claim.
  • Check the campaign cap and the brand window are sensible for the audience.
  • Confirm the suppression list includes recent opt-outs.

After every send:

  • Read the unsubscribe mailbox and the Activity log.
  • Suppress anyone who asked to stop, immediately.
  • Keep a record of consent or legitimate-interest decisions outside the app; the app stores the confirmation tick, not your legal reasoning.

What MarketPilot refuses to do

Not includedWhy
Scraping websites or social profilesEvery connector uses an official API. Audience data comes from a CSV you supply.
Purchased or rented listsThe app cannot make a list lawful, and importing one undermines every downstream guard.
Drip sequences and automatic follow-upsOne contact per campaign prevents accidental harassment.
Fake urgency, invented reviews or statisticsThe writer prompt forbids invented facts, prices and testimonials.
Hidden posting without approvalDry run, approval lock and campaign confirmation are on by default.
Uploading your images to a CDNInstagram and Google need a public URL; hosting that folder is your choice and your control.

Your side of the bargain

The app can enforce timing, caps and suppression. It cannot verify that a sentence is true, that a contact is lawful, or that a token is stored safely. Keep these habits:

  • Keep the brand profile current. The writer cannot see your calendar, inventory or prices. Stale offers become false advertising.
  • Read before approving. Especially offers, prices, health claims and legal claims.
  • Watch the Activity log. A WARN about retries is a signal, not noise.
  • Protect the Windows account. Credentials are plain text in %APPDATA%. Use a screen lock, a separate account for customer work, and never sync the settings file.
  • Respect platform rules. Rate limits, WhatsApp's 24-hour window and Instagram's image requirement are not obstacles to work around.

Going live is a deliberate act. Turn dry run off for one low-risk channel, publish one post, read the API response, then turn it back on until you trust the cadence. Repeat that habit for every new brand.
